Across the continent, the energy conversation is changing. The question is no longer _if_ renewables will dominate future capacity, but how quickly grids can modernize and how policy can unlock investment while protecting consumers.

## What's different now

Costs for utility-scale solar have fallen by more than 80% in a decade. Battery storage is following a similar curve. At the same time, population growth and rapid urbanization are pushing electricity demand faster than most national grids were designed to absorb.

- **Falling costs** for solar and storage make off-grid and mini-grid economics viable for millions of rural households
- **New financing models** — blended-finance structures, pay-as-you-go solar — have de-risked investment in last-mile electrification
- **Industrial demand** from mining, manufacturing, and data centers is pushing reliability requirements that renewables alone cannot yet fully meet without storage

## Gas as a bridge—or a trap?

Natural gas remains a contested topic. Proponents argue it is a necessary bridge fuel that provides dispatchable power while renewable capacity scales. Critics worry that new gas infrastructure locks in decades of carbon emissions and crowds out renewables investment.

The nuanced position adopted by most development finance institutions is that **gas can be justified where it displaces diesel or biomass**, but greenfield LNG terminals designed for export markets deserve more scrutiny.

## Grid modernization is the bottleneck

Even where generation is abundant—South Africa, Ethiopia, Egypt—transmission and distribution losses, ageing substations, and currency mismatches in power purchase agreements limit how much electricity actually reaches end users.

Regional power pools like the Eastern Africa Power Pool (EAPP) and the Southern African Power Pool (SAPP) are designed to allow countries to trade surplus power across borders. In practice, interconnectors are underfunded and political trust is uneven. Closing that gap is arguably the single highest-leverage infrastructure investment available.

## The path forward

The most credible roadmaps combine three tracks running in parallel:

1. **Utility-scale renewable generation** at sites with high solar or wind resource
2. **Mini-grid and off-grid expansion** for communities far from transmission infrastructure
3. **Grid hardening and smart metering** to reduce losses and enable demand-side management

None of these tracks is sufficient alone. Progress on all three simultaneously is politically and logistically harder—but that is the work.
