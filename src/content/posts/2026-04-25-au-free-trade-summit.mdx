Trade ministers from 38 African Union member states emerged from three days of closed-door negotiations in Addis Ababa on Thursday with a framework agreement on rules of origin that diplomats described as the most significant advance in AfCFTA implementation since the agreement entered into force.

Rules of origin—the technical criteria that determine whether a product qualifies for preferential tariffs under a trade agreement—had been the central sticking point blocking manufacturers from accessing the duty-free terms the AfCFTA nominally offers.

## What was agreed

The framework adopts a **product-specific rules** approach for manufactured goods, setting different local content thresholds by sector rather than a single blanket percentage requirement. The headline numbers:

- **Textiles and apparel**: 35% local value addition threshold, reduced from the previously proposed 45%, following intense lobbying from Ethiopian and Mauritius garment manufacturers
- **Automotive components**: 40% regional content, with a seven-year phase-in for countries without existing automotive supply chains
- **Processed foods and beverages**: 25% local agricultural input by value, with country-of-origin certification managed through a new digital certificate system piloted in East Africa
- **Pharmaceuticals**: 30% active pharmaceutical ingredient threshold, with exemptions for essential medicines under patent

The framework does not cover services, which account for a growing share of intra-African trade and remain subject to separate negotiations expected to conclude by mid-2027.

## Who pushed hardest for the deal

Nigeria and South Africa—the two largest economies and historically the most protective of their industrial bases—signalled openness to the lower threshold numbers in exchange for language on safeguard measures allowing temporary tariff reimposition if import surges threaten domestic industries.

Egypt and Morocco, both significant manufacturers with existing export-oriented industrial zones, pushed for the digital certificate system that simplifies compliance for mid-sized exporters who previously faced administrative costs that wiped out any tariff benefit.

Rwanda and Senegal, often described as AfCFTA's most enthusiastic implementers, provided the political energy that kept negotiations from collapsing when Nigeria and Egypt exchanged sharp words over pharmaceutical IP provisions on the second day.

## Implementation is the test

An agreement on rules of origin is necessary but not sufficient. Implementation will require:

**Customs capacity upgrades** in the 28 countries that still do not have electronic single-window systems capable of processing preferential origin certificates automatically. The AfCFTA Secretariat has requested $140 million from the AU Development Fund for this work over the next three years.

**Business awareness campaigns** targeting manufacturers who often do not know what they are eligible for under existing agreements, let alone new ones. Research by the International Trade Centre consistently finds that preferential tariff utilisation rates are low across African trade agreements because exporters simply do not apply.

**Dispute resolution** when countries disagree about whether a product meets origin criteria. The AfCFTA dispute settlement mechanism has not yet been tested on a major commercial dispute; several trade lawyers cautioned that its robustness is unproven.

## The bigger picture

If implemented well, the rules of origin framework could catalyse a significant shift in investment patterns. Manufacturers who previously had to choose a single African country as their production base and export to the rest of the world may now find it viable to serve multiple African markets from a regional hub with preferential access across borders.

That is the logic that made the EU single market transformative for European manufacturing. It took Europe three decades of painstaking legal and institutional construction. Africa is attempting something similar in a more compressed timeframe and with more diverse starting conditions.
